# Taste

The user's taste across projects, one rule per line with its reason. Anything tied to a project's palette, fonts or art style belongs in that project's taste file, and this public file names no project, client or person.

## Look
- Simple beats decorated: the smallest move that fixes the problem.
- Calm but never empty: thin screens read empty, busy ones overdone; stop a pass when it stops improving.
- A page keeps one layout across its states: fill an empty column instead of moving what sits beside it, which reads as a layout shift.
- A move that leaves half a section empty reads worse than today; sections the user already approved stay as they are.
- Keep the established frame; a new direction has to beat it clearly.
- Colour follows meaning: red is for danger or a miss and is never louder than its words.
- Square badges and dots read clunky, even in a square design.
- List rows stay plain: no action buttons inside a row that already opens its item.
- Controls side by side share one height.
- Signed-in and admin screens are dense and uniform, built from the project's component library; a wide table scrolls sideways inside its column; features with no backend are not shown.
- More to scroll shows as an edge shade, not an inner scrollbar or an arrow.
- Decorations stay in proportion to the text beside them and off text and controls, where they read as buttons.
- Plain names over themed ones.
- Check how major modern sites and shadcn blocks solve a pattern before inventing one; laptop dropdowns show a few rows and scroll inside.

## Phones
- A phone should feel like a sleek native app: panels opened from the nav cover the screen below it, never a floating box with the page at its sides; taps don't flash or wait; the main button sits in thumb reach.
- Every touch control has a 44px touch area (an invisible extension where it looks smaller); icon buttons keep their desktop size.
- Fix a jumping menu's placement instead of locking scroll.
- No icon-only control for something phone users wouldn't recognise.
- A hidden gesture such as swipe to delete gets a one-time hint and a visible control for mouse and keyboard.
- A bar pinned to the bottom stops pinning while a field above it has focus, or the keyboard hides what they type.
- Android Back closes an open overlay before it leaves the page.

## Motion
- Better motion, not more: it marks a moment someone is watching (something landing, a check drawing); content fading on a tab switch reads as lag.
- Animate the icon itself rather than a transition around a static icon.
- The control confirms itself (Copied, Saved); no toast on top.
- A named material (emerald, glass) means its texture in the site's palette, not a flat colour, and it goes on art, not on controls.
- Use whatever library fits the effect beside the project's main one; no heavy 3D model ships for decoration.
- Never remove or weaken motion the user has seen and not rejected; fix the side effect instead.
- Entrance tweens never pass over headings or controls, and everything looks finished as a still under reduced motion.
- No full-screen blend layers: one halved a page's frame rate.

## Copy
- An empty list says plainly what is empty plus a short dry joke, jokes don't share one shape, and search or filter misses stay plain.
- Copy holds only what a person needs to act: no taglines or explainer lines; why a step is locked, what to fix and what happens next are not explainer lines; buttons name the action; live numbers are linked, not copied.
- Labels are short and distinct, never literal descriptions or near-synonyms (Silent and Mute).
- Loading is quiet: a still placeholder or one Loading line, never both.

## How the user judges
- Checks don't stand in for their eye: a variant that passed every check was still called ugly.
- Small UX details matter as much as the big picture.
- They want as few questions as possible.
